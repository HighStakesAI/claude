# HOW TO SEND THE INTAKE FORM TO A NEW CLIENT

Internal. Two minutes per client.

---

## The one rule

**Never share the template itself.** `TEMPLATE — Client Intake (fillable · make a copy
per client)` stays private to us, forever. Every client gets their own copy.

If you share the template with two clients, they are editing the same document: client B
reads client A's revenue, license numbers, and competitor notes, and either one can
delete the whole thing. There is no undo for that conversation.

---

## Four steps

Everything lives in **My Drive → `00 — High Stakes AI Templates`**.

**1. Copy the template.**
Right-click `TEMPLATE — Client Intake (fillable · make a copy per client)` → **Make a
copy**. Name it `<Business Name> — Client Intake`.

**2. Move it into the client's folder.**
Drag it into `My Drive → <Business Name>`. If the folder does not exist yet, create it
with these subfolders — the form tells the client to use them by name:

```
<Business Name>/
├── <Business Name> — Client Intake   ← the copy
├── Logos/
├── Pics + Vids/
└── Licenses + Insurance/             ← §2.4 asks them to drop the COI here
```

**3. Fill in the cover row yourself before sending.**
Top of the form: *Prepared for* (their business name), *Date sent*, *Please return by*
(give a real date — "whenever" gets you "never"). Ten seconds, and it roughly doubles
the odds of getting it back.

**4. Share the folder — not just the document.**

Right-click the **`<Business Name>` folder** → **Share** → add the owner's email →
**Editor** → Send.

Share the folder rather than the file because the form asks them to upload things: logo
files, job photos, their certificate of insurance. If you share only the Doc, they have
nowhere to put any of it and you are back to chasing attachments over text message.

> **Set "General access" to Restricted** (specific people only). Never "anyone with the
> link." This document will contain their license numbers, revenue figures, and pricing.

---

## What to send with it

Short beats polished. Paste this into an email or a text:

> Hi [Name] — here's the onboarding form so we can get started on [the website / your
> Google profile / SEO].
>
> [link]
>
> It's longer than most, on purpose: everything in it is something we'd otherwise have to
> call you about halfway through the build. Most of it takes about 25 minutes.
>
> Two things:
> - The fields marked **[REQUIRED TO START]** are the ones we genuinely can't begin
>   without. The rest can follow this week.
> - **Don't put any passwords in it.** Section 14 shows you how to give us access
>   properly — you invite us as a user and you can remove us any time.
>
> If anything is confusing, skip it and write "don't know" — that's a useful answer and
> we'll figure it out on the kickoff call.
>
> — [You]

---

## When it comes back

1. **Check the 12 `[REQUIRED TO START]` fields first.** If any are blank, that is your
   one follow-up message — send it the same day, not next week.
2. **Read 14.12 before anything else.** If a former web guy still has access, that is
   this week's work, ahead of any build task.
3. **Read 9.5.** If the client does not control their own domain, stop and talk to them
   before you design a single page.
4. **Read 11.9.** A yes or a don't-know means a backlink audit before any SEO work.
5. **Save the licence numbers into the site build brief immediately** — every contractor
   page needs the number on it (Fla. Stat. §489.119(5)(b)).
6. Leave the completed form in the client folder. It is the record of what they told us,
   and the reason we published what we published.

## Sharing caveat worth knowing

Anyone you give **Editor** on a folder can see and delete everything inside it, including
anything you add later. Today the client folders only hold things the client gave us, so
this is fine. The day you start keeping internal drafts, pricing notes, or audit findings
in there, move them out — or keep them in a separate folder that is not shared.

To revoke later: right-click the folder → Share → click their name → Remove access. They
lose the form and its contents instantly, so export or copy it first if you still need it.
